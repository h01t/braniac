# PlayStation 5

**Summary**: Sony’s ninth-generation home console, featuring a custom AMD CPU/GPU, ultra-fast SSD, and innovative controller. Reviewed by Digital Foundry at launch in November 2020.
**Source Context**: “PlayStation 5 review: welcome to the next generation | Digital Foundry” (https://www.digitalfoundry.net/articles/playstation-5-review-digitalfoundry)

---

PlayStation 5 is powered by [[concepts/amd-custom-processor.md]] with a boost clock that fluctuates based on load, defined by a fixed power limit. Total system power draw peaks at about 200W under heavy game load (e.g., *Marvel’s Spider-Man: Miles Morales*), matching the peak of [[entities/xbox-series-x.md]] running *Gears 5*. The console is physically large to manage heat dissipation, using a large cooler and 120mm fan; thermal analysis showed surface temperatures in the mid‑30s °C at the core and up to 57 °C on the metal LAN port. Noise is described as “basically a non‑issue” under typical living‑room conditions.

The console boots from cold in less than 14 seconds, or about 7 seconds from rest mode. The UI renders at native 4K in HDR and offers activity cards for games. Settings allow a user to choose performance or quality mode for compatible titles. However, [[concepts/hdmi-2-1-vrr-and-120hz.md]] implementation is limited: 120 Hz output works only when a game enables it, and variable refresh rate (VRR) is not supported at launch. Auto Low Latency Mode (ALLM) is also absent. The console does not support native 1440p output; 1440p displays receive a downscaled 4K signal.

Storage is 825 GB (667 GB usable). A [[concepts/ps5-storage-expansion.md]] slot for PCIe 4.0 M.2 NVMe drives is present but not functional at launch pending Sony’s certification. External USB drives can store PS4 games but not PS5 titles.

The [[entities/dualsense-controller.md]] and [[concepts/tempest-3d-audio-engine.md]] are highlighted as key next‑gen features. In pack‑in title *Astro’s Playroom*, haptic feedback and adaptive triggers create a revolutionary tactile experience; effects in *Spider‑Man: Miles Morales* are more subtle but still natural. 3D audio via headphones adds immersion but, as of launch, does not surpass a traditional 7.1 surround system.

Backwards compatibility with PS4 games is excellent, with many titles (e.g., *Ghost of Tsushima*, *Days Gone*) already running at 60 fps.
