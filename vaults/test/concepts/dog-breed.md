# Dog Breed

**Summary**: A group of dogs that "breeds true" — producing consistent, predictable offspring — and is recognized by a kennel club such as the American Kennel Club.
**Source Context**: https://www.akc.org/dog-breeds/

---

A dog breed is defined by its ability to "breed true." Breeding a purebred Irish Setter to another purebred Irish Setter will always produce dogs instantly recognizable as Irish Setters (per AKC). This concept is the simplest way to distinguish a breed from a kind or type.

People have been breeding dogs since prehistoric times, starting with wolves. Early breeders selected for traits suited to hunting, guarding, and herding. Over thousands of years, humans bred toward physical and mental traits for specific jobs. For example, sleek Greyhound types were bred to chase fleet-footed prey, while huge mastiff types were used as guard dogs and warriors.

There are over 340 dog breeds known throughout the world. The American Kennel Club recognizes 200 breeds. Each recognized breed has a written [[concepts/breed-standard.md]] that sets forth ideal physical traits, movement, and temperament.
