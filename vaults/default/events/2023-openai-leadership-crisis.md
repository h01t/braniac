# 2023 OpenAI Leadership Crisis

**Summary**: In November 2023, OpenAI's board fired CEO Sam Altman, citing a lack of consistent candor, sparking employee revolt and instability before reinstating him days later after a deal with Microsoft and a board restructuring.
**Source Context**: https://grokipedia.com/page/OpenAI

---

## The Ouster

On **November 17, 2023**, OpenAI's board removed [[entities/sam-altman.md]] as CEO, citing his "lack of consistent candor in his communications with the board," which hindered its oversight duties. The board named Chief Technology Officer [[entities/mira-murati.md]] as interim CEO.

OpenAI President [[entities/greg-brockman.md]] stated the decision involved no financial, business, safety, security issues, or malfeasance by Altman. He then resigned in solidarity, noting the board had not consulted him.

## Underlying Tensions

The move exposed deep tensions between **Altman's push for rapid commercialization** and the **board's focus on AI safety and long-term existential risks**.
*   Board members, including co-founder and Chief Scientist [[entities/ilya-sutskever.md]], [[entities/tasha-mccauley.md]], and [[entities/helen-toner.md]], raised concerns about Altman's concentration of power and information withholding, such as details about [[entities/chatgpt.md]] incidents.
*   These differences aligned with [[concepts/effective-altruism.md]]'s emphasis on existential risks versus Altman's focus on scaling amid competition from investors like [[entities/microsoft.md]].

## Employee Revolt and Microsoft's Role

The ouster sparked massive internal instability. **Nearly all 770 OpenAI employees** signed a letter threatening to resign and join Altman at Microsoft unless he and Brockman were reinstated and the board changed.

Microsoft, with over **$13 billion invested** in OpenAI, prepared to hire the departing talent and form a new AI unit under Altman.

## Resolution and New Board

By **November 22, 2023**, OpenAI reinstated Altman as CEO and Brockman as President. The prior board was dissolved except for [[entities/adam-dangelo.md]]. A new initial board was formed with:
*   **Bret Taylor** as Chair
*   **Larry Summers**
*   **Adam D'Angelo**

Ilya Sutskever stepped back from operations and left OpenAI by May 2024.

## Aftermath and Review

A March 2024 review by the law firm **WilmerHale** found no misconduct by Altman justifying his removal, which later enabled his addition to the board. The crisis revealed governance flaws in OpenAI's nonprofit-capped-profit structure, leading to adjustments aimed at balancing commercial goals with safety oversight.
