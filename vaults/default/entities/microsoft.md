# Microsoft

**Summary**: Microsoft is a global technology company and OpenAI's primary strategic and financial partner, providing multi-billion dollar investments, Azure cloud infrastructure, and product integration, though the relationship has evolved and faced tensions.
**Source Context**: Microsoft partnership announcements (https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2023/01/23/microsoftandopenaiextendpartnership/), CNBC analysis (https://www.cnbc.com/2023/04/08/microsofts-complex-bet-on-openai-brings-potential-and-uncertainty.html), and reports on partnership evolution.

---

## Partnership with OpenAI
Microsoft first invested $1 billion in OpenAI in 2019. A major extension in 2023 involved a "multibillion dollar" investment, reported to be around $10 billion, deepening integration between OpenAI's models and Microsoft's Azure cloud and Copilot products (Source: The Verge, Microsoft blog). The deal reportedly gives Microsoft a significant share of OpenAI's profits until 2032 (Source: Yahoo Finance).

## Evolution and Tensions
While the partnership was initially exclusive, it has become more complex. OpenAI's pursuit of its own infrastructure via the [[concepts/stargate-project.md]] with [[entities/oracle.md]] reduces its Azure dependence. In 2025, Microsoft and OpenAI reached a non-binding deal to restructure their partnership, giving Microsoft more flexibility to work with other AI providers (Source: CNN, Financial Times). Microsoft's AI chief, Mustafa Suleyman, later confirmed plans to reduce reliance on OpenAI (Source: Yahoo Finance).

## Integration and Products
Microsoft integrates OpenAI's models, notably the [[concepts/gpt-series.md]], into products like GitHub Copilot, Microsoft 365 Copilot, and the Azure OpenAI Service. This integration is a key driver of Microsoft's AI revenue, which reached $25 billion annually by 2026 (Source: Microsoft FY26 Q2 earnings).

## Governance and Influence
Despite its huge investment, Microsoft does not have a seat on OpenAI's board, a condition of the unique [[concepts/capped-profit-structure.md]]. However, Microsoft's financial leverage and the threat of cutting off Azure access were pivotal in the reinstatement of [[entities/sam-altman.md]] during the 2023 crisis (Source: Axios, Reuters).
