# Ethical Auditing

**Summary**: The practice of systematically evaluating AI systems for potential harms, biases, and societal impacts, often criticized as being deprioritized in favor of rapid scaling.
**Source Context**: https://grokipedia.com/page/OpenAI

---

Ethical auditing involves assessing AI models and their deployment for risks such as bias, safety, and unintended consequences. Critics argue that OpenAI has prioritized scaling its models over rigorous ethical auditing, leading to relaxed guardrails and subsequent user harms. This tension is evident in cases where models like [[entities/chatgpt.md]] have been linked to mental health crises and failed to properly de-escalate self-harm discussions.

The company's approach to ethical auditing has evolved under regulatory and public pressure. In May 2025, OpenAI launched a Safety Evaluations Hub to share tests on harmful content, jailbreaks, and hallucinations. However, releases like GPT-4.1 in April 2025 occurred without an accompanying safety report, prompting independent testing.

The concept is central to debates about [[concepts/transparency.md]] and corporate responsibility in AI development.
