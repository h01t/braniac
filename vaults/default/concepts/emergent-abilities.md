# Emergent Abilities of LLMs

**Summary**: Capabilities in large language models that are not present in smaller-scale models and arise unpredictably as model scale increases.
**Source Context**: Comprehensive Overview of LLMs.pdf (References)

---

## Definition
Emergent abilities refer to skills or functionalities that appear suddenly and unpredictably in large language models when they reach a certain scale of parameters and training data. These abilities are not present in smaller variants of the same model family and are a key argument for the importance of scaling [[concepts/scaling-laws.md]].

## Examples from Literature
The reference list cites foundational work on this phenomenon:
*   **"Emergent abilities of large language models"** (Wei et al., 2022) is a seminal paper identifying and cataloging these abilities.
*   **"Emergent analogical reasoning in large language models"** (Webb et al., 2023) examines how complex reasoning can emerge.
*   **"Emergent autonomous scientific research capabilities of large language models"** (Boiko et al., 2023) explores advanced applications.

## Significance
The existence of emergent abilities underscores the discontinuous, non-linear benefits of scaling up model size. It suggests that simply making models larger can unlock qualitatively new behaviors, such as complex reasoning, step-by-step problem decomposition (chain-of-thought), and tool use, which are critical for advanced AI applications.
