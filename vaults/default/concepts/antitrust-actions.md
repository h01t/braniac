# Antitrust Actions

**Summary**: Google has been the subject of major antitrust lawsuits and regulatory actions in the United States and the European Union, resulting in findings of illegal monopolization in search and digital advertising markets, accompanied by fines and court-ordered behavioral remedies.
**Source Context**: https://grokipedia.com/page/Google

---

Google's dominant market positions have led to protracted legal battles with competition authorities alleging anti-competitive conduct.

## United States Department of Justice Lawsuits
The U.S. DOJ filed an antitrust lawsuit against Google in October 2020, alleging it maintained an illegal monopoly in general search services and search advertising via exclusive agreements with device manufacturers and browsers, including multi-billion dollar payments to Apple for default search placement.

*   **Search Monopoly Case:** In August 2024, the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia ruled that Google violated Section 2 of the Sherman Antitrust Act by monopolizing these markets. On September 2, 2025, the court imposed remedies requiring Google to share search data with competitors and prohibiting exclusive default agreements. Google appealed the ruling in January 2026.
*   **Ad Tech Monopoly Case:** In a separate action filed in January 2023, the DOJ accused Google of monopolizing digital advertising technologies. In April 2025, a U.S. District Court ruled that Google violated antitrust laws via anticompetitive conduct, including bundling services. Remedies in this case emphasized unwinding integrations without divestitures.

## European Commission Investigations
The European Commission has conducted multiple antitrust investigations since 2010, imposing fines exceeding €8 billion for abuses related to online shopping, Android licensing, and AdSense contracts. In September 2025, it fined Google €2.95 billion ($3.45 billion) for self-preferencing in ad technology since 2014, favoring its own tools like Google Ad Manager.

## Google's Defense and Market Context
Google and its supporters contend that its high market shares reflect superior product quality and user preference, not exclusion. They point to rivals like Bing, DuckDuckGo, and AI tools like ChatGPT as evidence of a dynamic market. Courts, however, have found willful maintenance of power through contracts and data advantages. The imposed remedies have generally prioritized behavioral changes (e.g., data sharing, contract prohibitions) over structural breakups like divestitures.
