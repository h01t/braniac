# Distillation

**Summary**: The process of transferring knowledge from a larger, more capable model (the teacher) to a smaller model (the student), used in DeepSeek-R1 to create powerful smaller reasoning models.
**Source Context**: DeepSeek_R1.pdf

---

## Role in DeepSeek-R1
The paper explores distillation as a method to propagate the reasoning capabilities of the large [[entities/deepseek-r1.md]] model to smaller dense models (e.g., Qwen2.5, Llama series). This results in a family of open-source models that retain strong reasoning performance at lower computational cost.

## Methodology
Distillation is performed via supervised fine-tuning: the smaller base models are trained on reasoning data generated by DeepSeek-R1. The paper notes that direct distillation from DeepSeek-R1 outperforms applying RL directly on the smaller models, indicating that the reasoning patterns discovered by the larger model are crucial.

## Results
The distilled models achieve exceptional performance on benchmarks:
- **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B**: 55.5% on AIME 2024, surpassing QwQ-32B-Preview.
- **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B**: 72.6% on AIME 2024, 94.3% on MATH-500, 57.2% on LiveCodeBench, comparable to o1-mini.
The paper open-sources distilled checkpoints at 1.5B, 7B, 8B, 14B, 32B, and 70B sizes.

## Significance
Distillation demonstrates that reasoning capabilities can be effectively transferred, making advanced reasoning accessible in smaller, more deployable models. It also shows that the [[concepts/chain-of-thought.md]] patterns learned by the teacher model are valuable for student models.

## Related Concepts
Distillation relies on the quality of the teacher's reasoning, which is developed through [[concepts/reinforcement-learning.md]]. It is an alternative to training smaller models via RL from scratch.
