# Google Corporate Governance

**Summary**: Google, under parent Alphabet Inc., is governed by a leadership structure historically defined by its founders and a dual-class share system that concentrates voting power.
**Source Context**: https://grokipedia.com/page/Google

---

## Leadership History
Google was founded in 1998 by [[entities/larry-page.md]] and [[entities/sergey-brin.md]]. In 2001, Eric Schmidt joined as CEO, forming a leadership triumvirate until 2011. Larry Page then became CEO until the 2015 formation of Alphabet Inc., when Sundar Pichai was named CEO of Google. In 2019, Pichai also became CEO of Alphabet, while Page and Brin stepped back from executive roles but retained board seats and controlling shares (Source refs: 101, 102, 103, 104).

## Board and Share Structure
Alphabet's board includes founders, Pichai, and independent directors. Corporate policies limit directors to four public boards and separate the chair and CEO roles (Source ref: 105). A defining feature is the dual-class share structure established at the 2004 IPO: Class A shares (one vote), founder-held Class B shares (10 votes), and non-voting Class C shares (introduced 2014). This allows Page and Brin to retain over 51% of voting power despite owning under 12% of equity, aligning with their IPO letter's emphasis on long-term decision-making (Source refs: 106, 107).
