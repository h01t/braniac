# Search Engine Bias

**Summary**: The allegation that search engine algorithms, particularly Google's, systematically favor or disfavor certain viewpoints, ideologies, or sources in their results, often with political implications.
**Source Context**: https://grokipedia.com/page/Google

---

Search engine bias refers to the concern that the automated systems ranking web content may produce results that are not ideologically neutral. This is a major point of contention regarding [[concepts/google-search-algorithm.md]].

**Research and Allegations**
Researcher [[entities/robert-epstein.md]] has been a prominent figure studying this phenomenon. His experiments, including the "search engine manipulation effect (SEME)," suggest that undecided voters' preferences can be shifted by 20% or more through deliberate, subtle tweaks to search result rankings. He testified before Congress in 2019 that Google's systems appeared to suppress pro-Republican autocomplete suggestions and search results during the 2018 U.S. midterm elections.

**Google's Position and Policy Shifts**
Google insists its algorithms prioritize relevance, quality, and safety, not political viewpoints. By 2025, the company had shifted some policies toward "free expression," easing post-2020 election fact-checking measures and ad bans on certain election-related claims.

**Ongoing Debate**
The debate persists between those who view content [[concepts/content-moderation.md]] as necessary for user safety and those who see it as unchecked control allowing for ideological bias. Mainstream analyses often downplay systematic bias, while independent studies and critics point to visibility gaps for conservative or alternative viewpoints.
