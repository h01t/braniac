# OpenAI Data Acquisition and Lawsuits

**Summary**: OpenAI faces numerous lawsuits alleging it infringed copyright by scraping vast datasets, including books and news articles, to train its models without permission, compensation, or transformative fair use justification.
**Source Context**: https://grokipedia.com/page/OpenAI

---

## Training Data Method

OpenAI trains its foundational models, like the [[entities/gpt-series.md]], on **vast datasets** from public internet sources, web crawls, and licensed content. It uses filtering techniques to prepare this data. Critics argue this method infringes copyright by learning from and potentially reproducing protected works without permission. OpenAI defends it as **fair use**, claiming the transformative nature of AI model training creates new works, not copies.

## Key Lawsuit: The New York Times v. OpenAI

A central lawsuit is ***The New York Times Company v. OpenAI and Microsoft*** (filed December 27, 2023).
*   **Allegation**: The Times accuses OpenAI of scraping millions of its articles to train [[entities/chatgpt.md]], resulting in verbatim outputs that harm the Times's licensing business. It alleges copyright infringement and DMCA violations.
*   **Status**: On March 26, 2025, a judge denied OpenAI's motion to dismiss, allowing the case to proceed to discovery on logs and training data.

## Consolidated Lawsuits and International Cases

By April 2025, **twelve U.S. lawsuits** against OpenAI and Microsoft—from authors, news outlets, and publishers—were consolidated in Manhattan federal court. Plaintiffs claim unauthorized copying of works, including those from the [[entities/books3.md]] dataset, for model training and monetization. Plaintiffs include authors **Sarah Silverman** and **Richard Kadrey** (who filed in August 2023).

Internationally, India's **ANI** news agency sued in January 2025 over unauthorized content use, reflecting broader data sovereignty issues.

## Legal Arguments and Status

OpenAI maintains that training on public data is **fair use** and seeks dismissals. Courts have rejected most early dismissal motions. In June 2025, one court dismissed claims related to metadata but allowed core copyright infringement allegations to proceed.

These cases highlight the debate over whether AI training **erodes incentives for content creation**. Plaintiffs cite output similarities to their works, while defenders analogize the practice to the **Google Books** project.

## Related Tension

Tensions with co-founder **Elon Musk** were revealed in a leaked February 2023 text exchange from court documents, where CEO Sam Altman expressed hurt over Musk's public attacks. Musk apologized but stressed the high stakes of their work for civilization.
