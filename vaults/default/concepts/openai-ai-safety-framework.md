# OpenAI AI Safety Framework

**Summary**: OpenAI's safety approach includes a stated mission, a multi-level AGI progress framework, the disbanded Superalignment project, a Preparedness Framework for risk evaluation, and various internal committees and public commitments.
**Source Context**: https://grokipedia.com/page/OpenAI

---

## Mission and Charter

OpenAI was founded in 2015 with the mission "to ensure that [[concepts/artificial-general-intelligence.md]] benefits all of humanity." Its [[entities/openai-charter.md]] commits to prioritizing safety and alignment, conducting open research unless risks demand secrecy, cautious deployment to prevent power concentration, and avoiding AI uses that harm humanity. It does not reference AI sentience or consciousness.

## AGI Progress Framework

OpenAI defines a five-level framework for [[concepts/artificial-general-intelligence.md]] progress:
1.  **Level 1**: Conversational AI (e.g., chatbots).
2.  **Level 2**: Human-level reasoning on novel tasks.
3.  **Level 3**: Independent multi-step agents.
4.  **Level 4**: Innovative invention generation.
5.  **Level 5**: AI organizations outperforming humans in most valuable work, nearing superintelligence.

## Superalignment Project

In July 2023, OpenAI launched the **Superalignment project**, dedicating 20% of its compute resources over four years to align superintelligent systems with human intent. Led by [[entities/ilya-sutskever.md]] and [[entities/jan-leike.md]], it focused on scalable oversight, automated alignment, and robustness testing. The team **disbanded in May 2024** after their departures, with members reassigned to broader safety efforts.

## Preparedness Framework

OpenAI uses the **Preparedness Framework** (published December 2023, updated April 15, 2025) for pre-deployment model evaluations. It assesses risks like cyber attacks, biological misuse, and persuasion that could lead to severe harms. The framework involves capability tests, mitigations, and re-evaluations to flag high-risk models for delayed release. Model system cards (e.g., for o1) report external red teaming, internal tests, and applied mitigations.

## Governance and Commitments

Following November 2023 leadership changes, OpenAI formed a **Safety and Security Committee**, grew technical safety teams, and gave its board veto power over high-risk releases. In May 2024, it signed **Frontier AI Safety Commitments** for responsible scaling, risk information sharing, and multi-stakeholder collaboration.

The company has reaffirmed allocating 20% of compute to safety research, updated its Model Spec with public input, and conducted joint evaluations with firms like [[entities/anthropic.md]]. However, the **AGI Readiness Team** for advanced safeguards disbanded in October 2024, shifting duties organization-wide.

## Usage Policies

OpenAI's Usage Policies were updated on January 29, 2025, and October 29, 2025, to establish a universal set of prohibitions across all products. Key prohibitions include harming people, violating privacy, endangering minors, and irresponsible use in high-stakes decisions (e.g., automating legal or medical advice without human review).

## Recent Safety Initiatives (Late 2025 - Early 2026)

*   Released **open-weight safety models** for harm classification tasks.
*   Recommended shared safety principles and research collaboration among frontier AI labs.
*   Expanded **third-party external testing** for model risk assessments.
*   On February 27, 2026, reached an agreement with the U.S. Department of Defense to deploy AI models in classified networks, with Pentagon-approved safeguards prohibiting domestic mass surveillance and requiring human responsibility in decisions involving force.
