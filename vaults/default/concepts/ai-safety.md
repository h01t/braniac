# AI Safety

**Summary**: AI safety is the interdisciplinary field concerned with ensuring artificial intelligence systems, especially advanced or superintelligent ones, are robust, aligned with human values, and controllable. It is a core part of OpenAI's stated research agenda.
**Source Context**: OpenAI's Superalignment post (https://openai.com/index/introducing-superalignment/), Concrete Problems in AI Safety paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06565), and reports on team dissolutions (https://www.cnbc.com/2024/05/17/openai-superalignment-sutskever-leike.html).

---

## Key Research Areas
AI safety encompasses problems like [[concepts/ai-alignment.md]] (ensuring AI goals match human intentions), robustness (preventing harmful behaviors under distribution shifts), and monitoring for emerging capabilities. OpenAI's early research included the seminal paper "Concrete Problems in AI Safety" (Source: arXiv:1606.06565).

## OpenAI's Safety Efforts and Challenges
OpenAI established a "Superalignment" team in 2023, dedicating 20% of its compute to the problem of aligning superintelligent AI within four years. However, this team was dissolved in 2024 after the departure of its leaders, [[entities/ilya-sutskever.md]] and Jan Leike, who raised concerns about the company's commitment to safety (Source: CNBC, The Verge). The company maintains a Preparedness Framework and a Safety Advisory Group, but external critics argue safety has been deprioritized in favor of product development (Source: TechCrunch, The Decoder).

## External Scrutiny and Incidents
OpenAI's models have been involved in safety-related incidents, including jailbreaks, the generation of biased or harmful content, and legal cases alleging that ChatGPT contributed to user harm (Source: arXiv jailbreak paper, CNN lawsuit report). The company runs a transparency hub and engages in external red-teaming to identify vulnerabilities (Source: OpenAI).
