# Google Global Infrastructure

**Summary**: Google operates a vast global infrastructure of data centers, fiber networks, and subsea cables to support its services, with a focus on energy efficiency and custom AI hardware.
**Source Context**: https://grokipedia.com/page/Google

---

## Data Centers
Google operates over 130 hyperscale data centers worldwide as of 2025, supporting search, cloud, and AI services. Key locations include the United States (Iowa, Oregon, Ohio), Europe, and Asia-Pacific (Source refs: 128, 83). Google Cloud specifically maintains 40 regions with 121 availability zones, plus 27 under construction, including the Bangkok region launched in January 2026 (Source refs: 81, 82). These facilities increasingly use renewable power, though AI demand pushes total energy consumption to several gigawatts (Source ref: 129).

## Network and Connectivity
Google's private network includes over 2 million miles of terrestrial fiber, 33 subsea cable systems, and more than 200 points of presence for edge caching (Source ref: 130). It has invested in over 30 subsea cables to reduce latency, including recent projects like Sol, Nuvem, Dhivaru, and TalayLink (completed 2025) (Source refs: 131, 132, 133, 134, 135). This backbone minimizes reliance on the public internet but faces risks from cable cuts and geopolitics (Source ref: 136).

## Hardware Supply Chain
A key component is Google's custom AI accelerator, the [[concepts/tensor-processing-unit.md]] (TPU), an ASIC introduced in 2015 and manufactured by global foundries (Source refs: 137, 138). TPUs power internal AI and Google Cloud offerings, with surging 2025 demand leading to partnerships like a multi-billion dollar expansion with Anthropic (Source refs: 139, 140). The broader supply chain for servers faces risks from component shortages, trade tensions, and logistics disruptions (Source refs: 141, 142).
