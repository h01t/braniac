# OpenAI Capped-Profit Structure

**Summary**: A hybrid corporate structure adopted by OpenAI in 2019, creating a for-profit subsidiary limited by a non-profit charter to balance fundraising with its original mission.
**Source Context**: https://grokipedia.com/page/OpenAI

---

In 2019, OpenAI announced a major shift from a pure non-profit to a "capped-profit" model. This structural change was designed to solve the problem of attracting the massive capital investment required for advanced AI research, particularly for computational resources, while attempting to maintain its founding commitment to broadly beneficial outcomes.

## Structure and Mechanics
The new structure created OpenAI LP, a for-profit, limited partnership. This entity is legally bound by the charter of the original OpenAI Nonprofit, Inc., which remains the controlling shareholder. The "cap" refers to a limit on returns for investors (e.g., early employees and venture firms like Microsoft); profits beyond a certain multiple of the original investment are directed to the non-profit. This model was intended to align investor incentives with the company's long-term, safety-oriented mission, rather than unlimited profit maximization.

## Motivations and Criticisms
The primary motivation was financial necessity. Building state-of-the-art [[concepts/gpt-models.md]] requires enormous computing power, which in turn requires significant venture capital. The non-profit model was seen as insufficient to compete with well-resourced corporate labs.
Critics argue the structure creates fundamental tensions and may be unstable. It places the non-profit board in the difficult position of potentially restraining the for-profit arm's commercial ambitions. This tension was visible during the 2023 [[concepts/openai-leadership-instability.md]], where the board's concerns about AI safety and pace of commercialization reportedly played a role. Some observers see the capped-profit model as a compromise that may ultimately favor commercial growth over strict adherence to its original non-profit mission.
