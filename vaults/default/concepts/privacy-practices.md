# Privacy Practices

**Summary**: Google collects extensive personal data across its services to power targeted advertising and product personalization, a practice that has drawn legal challenges, fines, and settlements over transparency, user consent, and data retention.
**Source Context**: https://grokipedia.com/page/Google

---

Google's business model is heavily reliant on the collection and analysis of user data, which raises significant privacy concerns and regulatory scrutiny.

## Data Collection Scope
Google collects extensive personal data, including search queries, location history, Gmail content, YouTube views, Android app usage, and device identifiers. Collection occurs both actively through user interactions and passively via background processes, such as location tracking even when services appear disabled. This data centralizes user profiles that underpin targeted advertising, which accounts for over 80% of Alphabet's revenue.

## Data Use and Controls
Data is used for service delivery, analytics, fraud detection, and machine learning training. Google asserts it does not sell personal information directly but monetizes it through its ad systems. Users have access to controls like My Activity for viewing/deleting history, Activity Controls to pause collection categories, and auto-delete settings. However, opt-outs require proactive steps, as defaults favor data collection. Initiatives like the Privacy Sandbox, which sought to replace third-party cookies, were retired in October 2025.

## Legal Challenges and Settlements
These practices have resulted in numerous legal actions:
*   A €50 million GDPR fine from France's CNIL in 2019 for transparency lapses in ad consent.
*   A 2021 ruling in Australia over misleading location data practices.
*   U.S. class actions alleging undisclosed data collection.
*   A $1.4 billion settlement with Texas in 2025 over unauthorized location tracking.
*   A $68 million resolution in January 2026 for Google Assistant privacy issues.

Critics argue Google's practices emphasize data maximization over minimization, favoring revenue and personalization over user control and privacy by default. Centralized data also heightens risks of breaches, as seen in the 2018 Google+ incident.
