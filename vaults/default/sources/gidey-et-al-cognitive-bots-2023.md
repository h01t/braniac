# Towards Cognitive Bots: Architectural Research Challenges (Gidey et al., 2023)

**Summary**: A paper that outlines the architectural design challenges and research directions for creating "cognitive bots"—software agents that emulate higher-level human cognitive skills for automation.
**Source Context**: Fundamentals of Building Autonomous LLM Agents.pdf (Ref 13: Gidey et al., 2023. Towards cognitive bots: Architectural research challenges. In: AGI 2023. Springer).

---

## Vision for Cognitive Automation
The paper argues for moving beyond simple scripted bots towards agents capable of learning, reasoning, and adapting in complex environments. It frames the goal as building **user-like bots** that can perform cognitive work, a theme also explored in their survey (Gidey et al., 2023, LOD). This vision aligns closely with the objectives of advanced [[concepts/autonomous-llm-agents.md]].

## Key Architectural Challenges
The authors identify research challenges at the intersection of software architecture and AI, likely including: how to formally specify and verify cognitive agent architectures, how to integrate learning and symbolic reasoning components, how to design for long-term adaptation and self-healing, and how to represent knowledge and affordances for interaction. This work connects the formal methods background of [[entities/habtom-kidane-gidey.md]] (e.g., FACTum Studio) with cutting-edge agent design.

## Relevance to LLM Agents
While not exclusively about LLMs, the cognitive bot architecture provides a rigorous framework that can inform the design of LLM-based agents. It emphasizes verifiability, robustness, and human-like cognitive capabilities—goals that the LLM agent community is actively pursuing through techniques like [[concepts/llm-planning.md]], tool use, and reflection.
