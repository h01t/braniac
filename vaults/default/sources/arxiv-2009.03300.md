# Measuring Massive Multitask Language Understanding

**Summary**: Introduces the MMLU benchmark, a test designed to evaluate the knowledge and reasoning abilities of language models across a wide range of subjects and difficulty levels.
**Source Context**: arXiv preprint arXiv:2009.03300 (2020).

---

## Benchmark Design
The [[entities/mmlu-benchmark.md]] (Massive Multitask Language Understanding) consists of 15,908 multiple-choice questions spanning 57 tasks in elementary mathematics, US history, computer science, law, and more. The tasks vary from requiring simple fact retrieval to complex multi-step reasoning.

## Motivation and Findings
The authors created MMLU to probe whether models possess broad, human-like understanding. Initial results showed that transformer-based models lagged far behind human expert performance, highlighting a significant gap in comprehensive knowledge acquisition. The benchmark has since become a standard for tracking progress in language model capabilities.

## Impact
MMLU is widely used to compare state-of-the-art models. Its comprehensive nature makes it a more robust measure of a model's general intelligence than narrower, task-specific benchmarks.
