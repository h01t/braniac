# Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Large Language Models: A Survey (Gao et al., 2024)

**Summary**: A comprehensive survey that categorizes and reviews the advancements, techniques, and applications of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in the era of large language models.
**Source Context**: Fundamentals of Building Autonomous LLM Agents.pdf (Ref 11: Gao et al., 2024. Retrieval-augmented generation for large language models: A survey. arXiv:2312.10997).

---

## Survey Scope
This survey likely covers the evolution of RAG from its origins (e.g., [[sources/rag-lewis-et-al-2021.md]]) to its current status as a vital technique for enhancing LLMs. It would categorize different RAG paradigms (e.g., naive RAG, advanced RAG, modular RAG), discuss retrieval strategies (dense, sparse, hybrid), methods for embedding and chunking, and ways to integrate retrieval with generation. It also probably addresses challenges like retrieval quality, context window limits, and evaluation.

## Importance for the Field
As RAG has become a standard tool in the AI engineer's toolkit, this survey serves as a central reference for researchers and practitioners. It synthesizes a rapidly growing body of work, providing clarity on best practices and open problems. For anyone building [[concepts/autonomous-llm-agents.md]], understanding the state-of-the-art in RAG is essential for designing effective memory and knowledge access modules.

## Connection to Agents
The survey directly supports the development of agents by detailing how to equip them with reliable, external knowledge sources—a core capability for overcoming LLM limitations and enabling trustworthy automation. It bridges the gap between the general [[concepts/retrieval-augmented-generation.md]] technique and its specific application in agent architectures.
