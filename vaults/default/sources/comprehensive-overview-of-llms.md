# A Comprehensive Overview of Large Language Models

**Summary**: A survey paper providing a broad and systematic overview of Large Language Model (LLM) research, covering background concepts, architectural innovations, training strategies, fine-tuning, efficiency methods, datasets, evaluation, applications, and challenges.
**Source Context**: arXiv:2307.06435v10 [cs.CL] 17 Oct 2024.

---

## Authors and Affiliations
The paper is authored by Humza Naveed, Asad Ullah Khan, Shi Qiu, Muhammad Saqib, Saeed Anwar, Muhammad Usman, Naveed Akhtar, Nick Barnes, and Ajmal Mian from various institutions including The University of Sydney, University of Engineering and Technology Lahore, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, University of Technology Sydney, CSIRO, King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals, SDAIA-KFUPM Joint Research Center for AI, The University of Melbourne, Australian National University, and The University of Western Australia (Source: Comprehensive Overview of LLMs.pdf).

## Purpose and Scope
This paper aims to consolidate the rapidly expanding literature on [[concepts/large-language-models.md]]. It is intended as a concise yet comprehensive reference for researchers and practitioners, summarizing key developments across multiple LLM-related topics to provide a "bigger picture" of advances in the field (Source: Comprehensive Overview of LLMs.pdf).

## Key Topics Covered
The paper organizes its overview into several major branches of LLM research: 1) Pre-Training, 2) Fine-Tuning, 3) Efficient LLMs, 4) Inference, 5) Evaluation, 6) Applications, and 7) Challenges (Source: Comprehensive Overview of LLMs.pdf, Figure 3). Specific sections delve into background concepts like [[concepts/tokenization.md]], [[concepts/positional-encoding.md]], and [[concepts/attention-mechanisms.md]]; architectural and training details of pre-trained models; fine-tuning and alignment; multi-modal and augmented LLMs; and datasets and benchmarking.

## Historical Context and Trends
The paper notes the historical progression in NLP from statistical to neural language modeling, then to pre-trained language models (PLMs), and finally to LLMs with tens to hundreds of billions of parameters. It highlights an increasing trend in the number of released LLMs and a shift towards instruction-tuned and open-source models (Source: Comprehensive Overview of LLMs.pdf, Figures 1 & 2). Early significant models like [[entities/t5.md]] and [[entities/mt5.md]] employed transfer learning, while [[entities/gpt-3.md]] demonstrated zero-shot task transferability.

## Notable Contributions
The authors summarize major findings from popular contributions and provide detailed discussions on key design and development aspects to help practitioners leverage the technology effectively. The paper also covers emergent abilities of LLMs, such as reasoning and in-context learning, attributed to their scale.
